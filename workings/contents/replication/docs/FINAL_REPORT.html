1 · The model

The model in one picture

Raw data runs through six stages in order. Each box names the choices that mattered most; each was tested before it was kept.

Clean the data

  • Exposure from dates
  • Sum insured ÷1,000 errors
  • 604 duplicate claims removed
  • Motor customer IDs padded
  • Timber T/W recode merged
  • R11 elevation in feet, year built 1900 fixed
  • Contacts excluded (a leak)

Develop the target

  • Incurred − recoveries
  • Reporting pattern by line × peril
  • Bornhuetter–Ferguson for slow perils
  • Bodily-injury lift switched off (A28)

Model each line

Buildings: XGBoost depth 3 + large-claim GLM + Pareto, with two peril interactions
Contents: one Tweedie GLM, seasonal exposure
Motor: spline GLM + large-claim GLM + Pareto

Claim history

  • Credibility (k + n) / (k + E)
  • Own line, other home line, motor/home
  • No look-ahead

Project to 2025

  • Log-linear calendar trend
  • Level ranges: bootstrap + Mack
  • Buildings level ×1.033; motor mean adjustment ×1.005

Test every change

  • Fit 2021–22 → score 2023
  • Fit 2021–23 → score 2024
  • Additions kept only if both improve; exceptions named in section 3
  • Optional additions: also significant

Every idea tested, and why it was kept or dropped

Each point is one change, placed by its effect on D² when scored on 2023 (across) and on 2024 (up). A tested addition is normally kept only in the top-right quadrant, and an optional addition also needs a significant two-year gain. Three kept points sit outside that quadrant. The timber merge (−0.00068 / −0.00043) was applied as a data correction (D-24). The motor large-claim split (−0.00098 / +0.00343) is the owner's design (D-15); it fails the two-year rule, and that decision is still open on #142. Motor credibility k = 4 (−0.00017 / +0.00131) was chosen by the pre-registered capped-claims rule (D-30). The shaded band marks ±0.0003, about the noise of refitting.

7 · Final review

What the final review found still missing

Eight independent reviewers, each with a different lens, searched for untested improvements and measured them on the backtest. A skeptic then re-measured each candidate and tried to refute it, and a completeness critic looked for what everyone missed. Of 24 raw ideas, 12 were merged or dropped, 9 of 14 survived the skeptics, and the critic added 2 data errors.

✓ Corrected (D-26)

R11 elevation was in feet

All 49,046 addresses in region R11 run 3.25–3.30 times every other region at every percentile. The other regions cap at exactly 60.0, and R11 at 196.9, which is 60 × 3.28. The models were pricing R11's low-lying homes as higher, and so safer from flood, than they are. Now converted to metres.

✓ Corrected (D-27)

Year built 1900 was a placeholder

19,014 addresses carry 1900, 10.8 times any neighbouring year. Their energy ratings and claim rates look like houses of every age, yet the models priced them as pre-1900. Now imputed from energy rating.

✓ Switched off (D-28)

The bodily-injury lift was wrong

D-11 lifts each bodily-injury year back to 2021's claim size, on the view that open claims are under-reserved. But closed claims fell by as much as open ones in 2023, so the fall is real. Without the lift, motor's 2025 rate falls from 627 to 581, the size of change the leaderboard indicated.

8 · Limits

What was not tested, and why

Not doneWhy
Paid and incurred development trianglesThe claims file is one snapshot at 31 December 2024 with no history of case reserves, so movement on reported claims cannot be observed. Only reporting triangles are possible, and those were used (chain ladder, BF, Mack).
True geocoding and spatial smoothingNo coordinates, postcode or street exist in the data. Weather-based proxies were tested instead (A26).
Random cross-validation foldsDeliberately avoided: mixing the scored year into training rewards fitting year-specific noise. It is why the team's tuned XGBoost looked good on its branch and failed here.
Tuning to the public leaderboardAvoided apart from one designed A/B on motor's level. The public board is a sample, and the private board decides.
Uncertainty in the Pareto tail fitOpen (#140): the 2025 ranges include development and trend error but not the tail shape.
Leak scan of categorical featuresOpen (#141): the numeric scan and the year-recode scan were done; the same-year against next-year test for categories was not.
Reverse-engineering the data generatorParked: renewal premiums rise by a fixed amount each year, but that mixes risk ageing with inflation, so it was not used as a trend signal.
Recoveries on open motor claimsOpen (#160): not among items 1–6. It corrects the target definition but is worth only about ±0.0001.
Motor large-claim split decisionOpen (#142): it fails 2023 (the year of the 11.9m claim) and is kept as your design choice.
9 · Final run

The final run: what was adopted

Each recommendation was built behind its own switch and tested under the usual rules. Buildings was tested over four paired XGBoost seeds with the tree count fixed, so seed noise could not pass for a gain. An independent code review found two defects, both fixed before the final run. The changes that passed were then combined in one labelled run.

Strongest case against: every change was found and judged on 2023 and 2024. Seeds, placebos, cross-year fitting and a pre-registered rule guard against that, but some optimism remains. The motor level now matches the leaderboard reading, which was itself fitted to the public sample; the private board may differ. The final candidate scored 0.12805 against 0.12753 for the A22 file, so the public board agrees in direction; one public score cannot attribute the gain to any single change.

10 · Statistical tests

Hypothesis tests of the final model (A29)

After the final run, every GLM in the model was put through formal tests: Type III tests of each term, chi-square tests of actual against expected by level, level merging and term dropping scored out of time, and a screen of every pair of terms for interactions. The adoption rules were fixed before the runs, and two independent code reviews checked the tests themselves.
